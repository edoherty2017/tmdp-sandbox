## 2. Training labels are a deterministic function of the classifier's own features — CV F1=0.998, the near-binary score distribution, and the calibration story are all circular by construction

**Severity after rebuttal:** major | **Lenses:** labeling circularity and information leakage, statistical validity, calibration claims, T-MDP empirical work | **Acknowledged in report:** no

### Criticism
Every branch of auto_label_event is computable from the feature vector, mostly from identically defined features: the malicious-iff-attack-EID rule (preprocessing.py:309, verified) mirrors feat__is_attack_eid over the same frozenset (:223-225); the EID 1/4688 branch is exactly a function of feat__is_suspicious_process, feat__in_baseline, and feat__has_obfuscated_command (verified :318-323 vs context_window.py:94-96); for EID 7 the loader synthesizes the label token 'signed=false' into the command-line text the label rule then checks (:117 vs :341). The classifier is trained and cross-validated on 'recover a boolean function of your own inputs' — F1=0.998 is a floor, not an achievement — yet the abstract, Related Work ('F1=0.997'), and README headline it without caveat even though Section 7.2 concedes the overlap. This determinism also manufactures the near-binary score distribution (only ~7/12,409 events in 0.1–0.9) that the paper misattributes to 'highly distinctive attack patterns in the OTRF corpus' (line 62): any well-fit model saturates to 0/1 when labels are a deterministic function of features. CV is further leaked: context-window features are extracted over the full shuffled corpus before folding, pairing near-duplicate same-session events across folds.

## 4. McNemar p-values computed with a mathematically wrong formula (2-df tail for a 1-df statistic); the correct test flips the paper's stated significance conclusion

**Severity after rebuttal:** major | **Lenses:** statistical validity, T-MDP empirical work, overclaiming and scholarly hygiene | **Acknowledged in report:** no

### Criticism
run_security_batch.py converts the continuity-corrected chi-square to a p-value via p = exp(-chi2/2) — the survival function of a chi-square with TWO degrees of freedom, not one (verified in code). Every p-value in the Section 6.3 table is reproduced exactly by this wrong formula (b=6: exp(-4.17/2)=0.125; b=3: 0.513; b=2: 0.779; b=4: 0.325). The correct 1-df p for b=6, c=0 is 0.041, and the exact binomial McNemar — the test Section 5.5 promises ('McNemar exact test') and the one actually used in the file-deletion domain — gives p=0.031. At face value, two of the five rows the paper declares non-significant ARE significant at alpha=0.05, so the repeated conclusion 'not statistically significant at n=500' (lines 18, 336, 558) rests on p-values inflated ~3x by a coding error, with the exact test applied only in the domain where the method wins. Compounding this: no multiplicity correction anywhere despite ~10 hypothesis tests, and the five sigma levels reuse the same 500 scenarios with seed=scenario.seed+step, so the sweep is one correlated experiment counted five times — which is exactly why the b values failed to reproduce.
